# CHAPTER I

**THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND**

This chapter presents the background and related literature of the study entitled “Intelligent Property Management Workflow with OCR-Enabled Automated Billing Classification and Tenant Notification.” It also presents the significance of the study, the statement of the problem, and the scope and limitations of the system.

## 1.1 Introduction

Property management involves maintaining tenant records, managing utility bills, computing charges, monitoring payment status, and providing billing information to tenants. These activities require the organized handling of property records, tenant information, billing documents, and other administrative records. Cammarata (2023) discusses the importance of records and information management in real-estate operations, while Teo et al. (2024) examine property-management functions and services. When property managers handle these activities through separate or manual methods, organizing information and maintaining consistent work processes can become more difficult.

To support these activities, digital approaches provide a way to centralize property information and organize regular management tasks. Property Technology (PropTech) refers to the use of digital technologies in real-estate and property-management activities. Siniak et al. (2020) discuss the role of PropTech in changing real-estate operations and services, while Oyetunji et al. (2025) examine digital solutions for streamlining property-management operations in affordable-housing communities. Their discussion highlights how digital tools may support routine management activities, resource allocation, and tenant interaction, although the study also notes barriers such as financial constraints, infrastructure gaps, and resistance to change. Overall, this literature shows how digital technologies support property and housing-management activities and provides a basis for using centralized systems to organize property information and management processes.

One important area in property management is billing. Utility billing requires information from billing documents before charges can be processed, including amounts, dates, billing periods, providers, and account or reference identifiers. Optical Character Recognition (OCR) provides a way to extract text and relevant information from supported documents such as receipts and invoices (Kumar et al., 2020). Saout et al. (2024) discuss invoice-data extraction as a process involving document digitization, OCR, information extraction, and recognition of document structures. Mohan (2023) also presents a staged pipeline for extracting key-value information from commercial invoices. Overall, these studies support the use of OCR and document-processing methods to extract and organize information from billing documents for later processing.

After billing information is extracted, it still needs to be validated, classified, and processed according to defined billing conditions. Rule-based processing uses predefined conditions and logical rules to support structured operational decisions and information handling. Baralis et al. (2022) discuss business-rule engines that represent and execute operational decisions in a structured way. In relation to process automation, Geyer-Klingeberg et al. (2024) present a rule-based method that analyzes decision logic and classifies business processes according to their suitability for robotic process automation. These ideas are relevant to a billing workflow in which validation, classification, calculation, and exception-handling conditions are defined in advance. The reviewed literature provides a basis for combining OCR, rule-based processing, and notification technologies in a billing workflow for smaller residential property-management settings.

Based on these ideas, the study proposes a web-based workflow that helps property managers organize billing information and allows tenants to view their bills and history. The system receives supported utility-bill emails and documents, extracts available information through text parsing or OCR, and uses predefined rules to process valid records. Staff investigates records that require review, while the Manager handles decisions requiring financial authority. In this study, "intelligent" refers to OCR-assisted extraction and rule-based processing rather than autonomous decision-making. The workflow separates Manager-confirmed rent payments from personal utility tracking and provides permitted Web Push alerts and supplementary rent email notifications that direct tenants to the authenticated portal.

## 1.2 Review of Related Literature

### Digital Property Management and PropTech

Digital property management increasingly relies on technology to organize property details, management activities, billing data, and related services. Several studies have examined this shift from different perspectives: Teo et al. (2024) discuss technology and digitalization in property-management functions and service delivery, while Ding and Sun (2024) review technology innovations in housing management. In this context, PropTech refers to the use of digital technologies in the real estate and property-management sector. Siniak et al. (2020) examine the impact of PropTech on real estate industry growth, Saputra et al. (2023) review digital transformation trends in the property-management industry, and Al-haimi et al. (2025) synthesize the technologies, benefits, and challenges associated with digital transformation in real estate. In addition, Cammarata (2023) discusses the importance of records and information management in managing risks during real-estate changes. Overall, this literature supports the relevance of centralized digital tools for property information and administrative processes. Building on this literature, the present study proposes a centralized web-based workflow that organizes property, unit, tenant, utility, billing, payment, exception, and receipt information for selected boarding houses and residential apartments.

### OCR and Billing Document Extraction

Within property-management operations, billing is one area where digital processing can support routine administrative work. Billing documents contain information such as amounts, dates, billing periods, providers, and account or reference identifiers. Kumar et al. (2020) examine the extraction of information from bill receipts using Optical Character Recognition (OCR), while Saout et al. (2024) provide an overview of invoice-data extraction involving document digitization, OCR, information extraction, and document-structure recognition. Similarly, Mohan (2023) presents an end-to-end pipeline for custom key-value extraction from commercial invoices. However, extracting information from a billing document is only the first stage of billing processing. In the proposed system, readable text from supported billing emails and digitally generated PDFs is extracted directly, while clear bill images undergo OCR. After extraction, the information undergoes validation before billing processing. Records that do not meet the required validation conditions are sent to the Exception Queue for Staff investigation and, when required, Manager decisions. Thus, text extraction and OCR provide information for subsequent checks rather than a guarantee that every extracted value is accurate. The OCR component may employ machine-learning-based text-recognition technology to convert visible characters and document text into machine-readable output. However, the proposed system does not use OCR output or machine-learning recognition to determine billing validity, classify payment status, compute charges, or make property-management decisions.

### Rule-Based Billing Processing

Once billing information has been extracted, it must be checked and processed according to defined conditions. Rule-based processing uses explicit conditions and decision rules to classify, validate, and route information. Wu et al. (2022) demonstrate the use of rule-based information extraction to organize domain-specific information, while Baralis et al. (2022) discuss business-rule engines for representing and executing operational decisions in a structured manner. Extending this idea to process automation, Geyer-Klingeberg et al. (2024) present a rule-based method that analyzes decision logic and classifies business processes according to their suitability for robotic process automation, while Kim et al. (2011) examine proactive exception handling in business processes. These studies support the use of predefined and traceable conditions to determine whether a record proceeds through normal processing or requires additional review. Applying these principles, the proposed system uses defined rules for billing validation, provider and account/reference mapping, duplicate detection, and identifying individual or shared billing arrangements. Records that do not meet the defined conditions are sent to the Exception Queue for Staff investigation and, when required, Manager decisions. These rules, together with the separation between Tenant-reported rent and Manager-confirmed rent status, are system requirements rather than findings established by the cited studies. Digital rent receipts are created only after Manager confirmation. Personal utility tracking remains separate and does not establish provider settlement.

### Shared Utility Billing and Cost Allocation

Shared utility billing presents a related issue because a single charge may need to be allocated among several occupants. Levinson and Niemann (2004) examine apartment energy use when landlords pay for utilities, while Mayr and Souza (2025) discuss coordination and incentive issues associated with shared heating costs. These studies provide context for understanding cost responsibility and consumption incentives in shared-utility arrangements. Nevertheless, they do not establish one universal allocation formula for rental properties or determine how the proposed system should calculate individual tenant charges. For this reason, the proposed system treats the allocation method as a defined system requirement rather than as a formula established by the reviewed literature. For shared occupancy, charges are split equally among eligible tenants in a Manager-configured group. The billing requirements also include reconciliation controls so that allocated charges equal the source billing amount. The allocation formula, rounding procedure, and reconciliation method are described in the succeeding chapters and will be tested during system evaluation.

### Web Push, Rent Email Notifications, and Secure Notification Delivery

After a billing record is finalized, tenants need to be informed that their billing information is available. The World Wide Web Consortium (2024a) describes the Push API, including push services and user subscriptions. The World Wide Web Consortium (2024b) also explains the role of service workers in handling web events in the background when they are included in the system implementation. Because Web Push involves browser subscriptions and authorization, security must also be considered. Carboneri et al. (2023) examine Web Push implementations in the wild and identify security concerns involving push subscriptions and authorization. In the proposed system, Web Push Notifications serve as billing alerts that direct tenants to the authenticated web portal, where complete billing information is available. However, Web Push does not confirm payment, prove that payment occurred, or show whether the tenant read the notification. Tenant payment reporting therefore remains separate from Manager confirmation.

Email provides an additional channel for rent-related alerts. Klensin (2008) describes Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP) as a protocol for transporting electronic mail. Moore and Newman (2018) explain the use of Transport Layer Security (TLS) to protect email submission and access connections. These standards provide a technical basis for supplementary rent email notifications in the proposed system. Emails will inform tenants of rent billing updates, eligible reminders, and Manager-confirmed rent payments, and will direct them to the authenticated portal. Sending attempts and available acceptance or failure results will be recorded. Email-server acceptance will not prove inbox delivery or readership, and sending an alert will not change rent payment status.

### Iterative Software Development

Because the proposed system contains several connected functions, its development requires an organized and adaptable process. Iterative software development divides development into repeated cycles of building, reviewing, receiving feedback, and improving. Larman and Basili (2003) explain the development of iterative and incremental approaches, in which software is developed progressively through repeated cycles. Inayat et al. (2015) emphasize stakeholder involvement, communication, and requirements refinement in iterative requirements engineering, while Ramesh et al. (2021) identify challenges involving changing requirements, stakeholder participation, documentation, and communication in Agile software development. These studies support the use of Iterative Software Development Methodology in the present study. Although the literature supports this approach, the actual quality and performance of the proposed system will be determined through implementation, testing, user feedback, and evaluation.

### Software Quality Evaluation and Security

To evaluate the proposed system systematically, the study requires defined software-quality criteria. ISO/IEC 25010:2023 provides a product-quality model for systems and software quality requirements and evaluation (International Organization for Standardization, 2023). Guided by this model, the study will evaluate five quality characteristics: Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Interaction Capability, Reliability, and Security. These characteristics provide a basis for examining whether the system performs its intended functions, performs efficiently, supports user interaction, operates consistently, and protects information. Security is particularly important because the system handles tenant and billing information. Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012, requires organizations that process personal information to use reasonable and appropriate measures to protect it (Republic of the Philippines, 2012). The National Privacy Commission (2016) further discusses safeguards for the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal data, while Zafar et al. (2022) review authorization and access-control strategies that restrict system functions and information according to user permissions. In the proposed system, role-based access separates Admin maintenance, Staff operations, Manager authorization, and Tenant-own functions. Rent email notifications will use authorized recipient records, protected sending credentials, and limited message content that directs tenants to the authenticated portal. These sources guide the study’s quality and security evaluation, but they do not prove that the system already meets the selected quality characteristics or privacy requirements. The Security evaluation is limited to the defined system functions, access controls, and security-related criteria included in the study’s evaluation procedures. It does not constitute a legal audit, certification, or claim of complete compliance with the Philippine Data Privacy Act.

## 1.3 Significance of the Study

The study develops and evaluates a web-based property-management workflow that uses OCR-based information extraction, rule-based billing classification, Web Push Notifications, and supplementary rent email notifications. It primarily benefits property managers, operational Staff, and tenants and may serve as a reference for future researchers and system developers.

In the global context, the study contributes an applied example of PropTech and digital property management by showing how existing technologies can be combined into one workflow for residential property operations. It also provides a local example of how digital property-management concepts can be applied in a smaller property-management setting. The study does not claim that the proposed workflow represents all property-management environments; rather, its significance lies in demonstrating how selected technologies may be integrated within the defined scope of the study.

In the economic context, the system is designed to reduce repetitive manual activities involved in receiving utility bills, recording billing information, calculating tenant charges, and communicating billing updates. Centralized records and predefined billing rules can also help property managers follow a more consistent billing process. Processing time will be assessed during system evaluation. The study does not establish reductions in operating costs or late payments.

In the environmental context, the system uses electronic utility bills, digital billing records, and electronic notifications. This can reduce the need for paper-based records during the billing process. The study does not claim a specific reduction in paper use because this is not directly measured.

In the societal context, the system provides property managers and tenants with organized billing information. Itemized billing records, defined billing rules, and automated notifications can help make tenant dues easier to understand and communicate. This can support clearer billing transactions between property managers and tenants. However, the study does not claim that the system will eliminate billing misunderstandings or disputes because those outcomes are not directly measured.

## 1.4 Statement of the Problem

This study develops and evaluates an Intelligent Property Management Workflow with OCR-Enabled Automated Billing Classification and Tenant Notification for selected boarding houses and residential apartments in Pasig City.

Specifically, the study seeks to answer the following questions:

1. What development stages are undertaken in developing the proposed system using the Iterative Software Development Methodology?

2. How does the proposed system integrate OCR, rule-based billing processing, exception handling, payment management, digital receipts, Web Push Notifications, and supplementary rent email notifications into one property-management workflow?

3. How does the developed system perform when evaluated using the selected ISO/IEC 25010:2023 quality characteristics of Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Interaction Capability, Reliability, and Security?

4. What implementation guide and user manual can be prepared for the developed system?

## 1.5 Scope and Limitations of the Study

The study will develop and evaluate a web-based property-management workflow for selected boarding houses and residential apartments in Pasig City during Academic Year 2026-2027. It will centralize property, unit, tenant, utility-mapping, rent, billing, payment, exception, correction, receipt, and notification records. “Intelligent” refers to OCR text extraction combined with explicit, deterministic, and traceable processing rules. An existing OCR engine may use machine-learning-based recognition, but the researchers will not train or customize a model. Validation, classification, computation, allocation and payment decisions will follow predefined rules and Manager-controlled actions rather than autonomous AI decisions.

Billing will cover rent, electricity, and water only. Rent will be fixed and non-prorated, generated once per monthly anniversary cycle with the same due date. Short months will use their final day while preserving the original anniversary day for later months. After an outage, missed eligible cycles will be generated once with their original due dates and actual creation dates retained. The first bill will show current rent and one-month advance separately. Confirmed advance will be reserved for the final applicable rent cycle and applied only with Manager confirmation, using the original payment and receipt without another cash transaction or duplicate receipt. Applied credit cannot be reused, and no replacement advance will be charged automatically. Individual charges will follow the configured lease or utility account. Shared charges will be split equally among eligible tenants in the Manager-configured group, with two-decimal shares totaling the source charge. Tenants will choose the payer of a shared provider bill outside the system; allocation will not represent contribution collection or verified provider settlement.

The system will support month-to-month rentals that continue until the Tenant leaves. The Manager will record the rental start date and leave the end date blank while occupancy continues. The end date will be recorded when the Tenant leaves. Rent will follow the monthly anniversary rule, with no new cycles generated after the recorded end date. The existing Tenant account will retain access to its own bills, utility tracking history, confirmed payments, and receipts, and unpaid balances will not be removed merely because occupancy has ended. Departed tenants may report outstanding rent payments for Manager confirmation. Advance rent will equal one month of the Tenant's configured rent or, for shared rent, the Tenant's calculated rent share at the time the advance is charged. Its amount will not be entered as an arbitrary credit allowance. A later change in rent or shared allocation that causes a credit mismatch will require Manager review. Automatic unit transfers and lease-renewal processing will not be included in the study.

Utility bills will enter the system through supported email-body summaries and attachments received by the designated property-management email account, or through optional Staff or Manager uploads of clear photographs or scans of printed provider bills. The Manager may hand a printed bill directly to tenants without uploading it. Until a supported email or uploaded copy is received and validated, that bill will not be available in the portal for automatic allocation, personal tracking, history, or reminders. Readable email summaries and digitally generated PDFs will use direct text extraction, while clear bill images will use OCR. The five target fields will remain the amount due, due date, billing period, provider, and account or reference identifier. Processing will be limited to selected provider formats; the availability of a provider email, PDF attachment, or complete identifier will not be assumed. Required-field, mapping, period, and duplicate checks will precede classification and computation across all supported intake methods. Masked or incomplete identifiers will require Manager review against verified account information; the system will not infer hidden characters or assign a bill by guessing. Incomplete, uncertain, duplicated, unmappable, or otherwise invalid records will enter the Exception Queue for Staff investigation or Manager correction. Staff correction proposals will require Manager authorization, and accepted corrections will undergo system revalidation and reprocessing. Finalized corrections will preserve prior values and tracking history. Utility due dates will come from validated received billing information; the system will not predict the next billing period's due date.

Rent payment statuses will remain Unpaid, Tenant Reported Paid, and Paid. Tenants may report rent payments for Manager review. The Manager may also record and confirm an actual rent payment without a prior Tenant report, after checking it outside the system. Only the Manager may authorize confirmed rent receipts; rejected reports will return to Unpaid with a recorded reason. Utilities will use a separate Tenant-controlled tracker with Not Marked Paid and Self-Reported Paid, accompanied by “Provider payment not verified.” A mark will refer only to the Tenant's own utility bill or allocated share and will neither require Manager confirmation nor generate a system-issued utility receipt. For shared bills, one Tenant's mark will not mark other shares or the whole provider account as paid. The system will not independently verify payments. Tenants will access their billing history, rent statuses, utility tracking records, and confirmed rent receipts through the authenticated portal.

Portal notifications will retain billing alerts that tenants can view after login. Web Push will provide rent and utility billing updates and eligible reminders. Supplementary email notifications will be limited to rent billing updates, eligible rent reminders, and Manager-confirmed rent payment notices sent to the Tenant's registered email address. Both channels will direct tenants to the authenticated portal; emails will not create another payment or receipt. Reminders will be scheduled at 9 AM Philippine time, three calendar days before the actual due date, for eligible Unpaid rent and utility records Not Marked Paid. Pending rent reports and utilities marked Self-Reported Paid will be excluded; utility reminders will use Web Push only. Bills unavailable before the scheduled reminder time, including rent generated on its due date, will receive billing updates rather than backdated reminders. A missed reminder will not be sent retrospectively. Email sending will require internet connectivity, configured sending credentials, and a running backend. Sending attempts and available channel acceptance or failure results will be recorded, with limited retries and duplicate prevention. Push permission, device support, unavailable email configuration, or delivery failure will not prevent portal access. An alert will not prove payment, inbox delivery, or readership. Tenant SMS and utility email notifications are excluded.

Admin access will be limited to account and system maintenance, including authorized role and workspace assignments. The Manager will establish properties, billing-linked units and occupancy, verified utility mappings, rent settings, and individual or shared arrangements; handle sensitive corrections; confirm or reject rent payments; authorize eligible final-cycle advance application; and access confirmed rent receipts. The Manager may also perform operational tasks. Staff will work only within the assigned business workspace, update permitted descriptive details on existing records, upload utility bills, monitor intake, investigate exceptions, and record notes or escalations. Staff may submit one pending correction proposal per unfinalized incoming utility bill, with proposed values and a reason. The proposal will not alter live billing until the Manager authorizes it and the system confirms that the source and relevant billing context remain current and pass revalidation. Rejection, stale proposals, and failed validation will retain reasons and leave the proposed billing change unapplied. Previous values and the preparing and authorizing actors will be retained. Staff will not create billing-linked occupancy, modify login identity or financial settings, confirm rent, apply advance, or change Tenant utility tracking. Routine valid bills will continue automatically without Staff or Manager approval. Tenants will view their permitted records, report rent payments, maintain their own utility tracking marks, and receive permitted alerts. Authentication and role restrictions will protect centralized records. Evaluation will cover Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Interaction Capability, Reliability, and Security under the selected ISO/IEC 25010:2023 characteristics. It will not establish long-term changes in revenue, profitability, payment compliance, or Tenant behavior.

Independent business workspaces will separate each management group's operational records. Managers and Staff will access only their authorized workspace, while Tenants will access their own records. Admin access will support authorized account and workspace maintenance without operational rent-confirmation authority. The Tenant calendar will display actual validated bill dates and clearly labelled estimates of upcoming configured rent dates. Estimates will not create charges, establish payment obligations, or predict utility dates. Workspace separation and calendar presentation support the included billing workflow rather than introduce new research objectives.

The study excludes mobile applications; installation or physical reading of smart meters or submeters; Internet or Wi-Fi billing; predictive profit analytics or billing; Generative AI and autonomous AI billing decisions; blockchain; automatic utility disconnection; payment gateways or bank integration; independent external payment verification; automatic access to provider apps or phone notifications; maintenance tickets; legal contract generation; and automated eviction. OCR will not cover handwritten, heavily damaged, severely distorted, or low-resolution documents. The study does not guarantee Web Push or email delivery or readership, establish a universally fair utility-allocation formula, or provide complete legal certification under the Philippine Data Privacy Act.
