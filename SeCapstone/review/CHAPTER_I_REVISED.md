# CHAPTER I

**THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND**

This chapter presents the background and related literature of the study entitled “Intelligent Property Management Workflow with OCR-Enabled Automated Billing Classification and Tenant Notification.” It also presents the significance of the study, the statement of the problem, and the scope and limitations of the system.

## 1.1 Introduction

Property management involves maintaining tenant records, managing utility bills, computing charges, monitoring payment status, and providing billing information to tenants. These activities require the organized handling of property records, tenant information, billing documents, and other administrative records. Cammarata (2023) discusses the importance of records and information management in real-estate operations, while Teo et al. (2024) examine property-management functions and services. When property managers handle these activities through separate or manual methods, organizing information and maintaining consistent work processes can become more difficult.

To support these activities, digital approaches provide a way to centralize property information and organize regular management tasks. Property Technology (PropTech) refers to the use of digital technologies in real-estate and property-management activities. Siniak et al. (2020) discuss the role of PropTech in changing real-estate operations and services, while Oyetunji et al. (2025) examine digital solutions for streamlining property-management operations in affordable-housing communities. Their discussion highlights how digital tools may support routine management activities, resource allocation, and tenant interaction, although the study also notes barriers such as financial constraints, infrastructure gaps, and resistance to change. Overall, this literature shows how digital technologies support property and housing-management activities and provides a basis for using centralized systems to organize property information and management processes.

One important area in property management is billing. Utility billing requires information from billing documents before charges can be processed, including amounts, dates, billing periods, providers, and account or reference identifiers. Optical Character Recognition (OCR) provides a way to extract text and relevant information from supported documents such as receipts and invoices (Kumar et al., 2020). Saout et al. (2024) discuss invoice-data extraction as a process involving document digitization, OCR, information extraction, and recognition of document structures. Mohan (2023) also presents a staged pipeline for extracting key-value information from commercial invoices. Overall, these studies support the use of OCR and document-processing methods to extract and organize information from billing documents for later processing.

After billing information is extracted, it still needs to be validated, classified, and processed according to defined billing conditions. Rule-based processing uses predefined conditions and logical rules to support structured operational decisions and information handling. Jiménez and Corchuelo (2022) discuss business-rule engines that represent and execute operational decisions in a structured way. In relation to process automation, Bédard et al. (2024) present a rule-based method that analyzes decision logic and classifies business processes according to their suitability for robotic process automation. These ideas are relevant to a billing workflow in which validation, classification, calculation, and exception-handling conditions are defined in advance. The reviewed literature provides a basis for combining OCR, rule-based processing, and notification technologies in a billing workflow for smaller residential property-management settings.

Based on these ideas, the study proposes a web-based workflow that helps property managers organize billing information and allows tenants to view their bills and history. The system receives supported utility-bill emails and documents, extracts available information through text parsing or OCR, and uses predefined rules to process valid records. Staff investigates records that require review, while the Manager handles decisions requiring financial authority. In this study, "intelligent" refers to OCR-assisted extraction and rule-based processing rather than autonomous decision-making. The workflow separates Manager-confirmed rent payments from personal utility tracking and provides permitted Web Push alerts and supplementary rent email notifications that direct tenants to the authenticated portal.

## 1.2 Review of Related Literature

### Digital Property Management and PropTech

Digital property management increasingly relies on technology to organize property details, management activities, billing data, and related services. Several studies have examined this shift from different perspectives: Teo et al. (2024) discuss technology and digitalization in property-management functions and service delivery, while Ding and Sun (2024) review technology innovations in housing management. In this context, PropTech refers to the use of digital technologies in the real estate and property-management sector. Siniak et al. (2020) examine the impact of PropTech on real estate industry growth, Saputra et al. (2023) review digital transformation trends in the property-management industry, and Al-haimi et al. (2025) synthesize the technologies, benefits, and challenges associated with digital transformation in real estate. In addition, Cammarata (2023) discusses the importance of records and information management in managing risks during real-estate changes. Overall, this literature supports the relevance of centralized digital tools for property information and administrative processes. Building on this literature, the present study proposes a centralized web-based workflow that organizes property, unit, tenant, utility, billing, payment, exception, and receipt information for selected boarding houses and residential apartments.

### OCR and Billing Document Extraction

Within property-management operations, billing is one area where digital processing can support routine administrative work. Billing documents contain information such as amounts, dates, billing periods, providers, and account or reference identifiers. Kumar et al. (2020) examine the extraction of information from bill receipts using Optical Character Recognition (OCR), while Saout et al. (2024) provide an overview of invoice-data extraction involving document digitization, OCR, information extraction, and document-structure recognition. Similarly, Mohan (2023) presents an end-to-end pipeline for custom key-value extraction from commercial invoices. However, extracting information from a billing document is only the first stage of billing processing. In the proposed system, readable text from supported billing emails and digitally generated PDFs is extracted directly, while clear bill images undergo OCR. After extraction, the information undergoes validation before billing processing. Records that do not meet the required validation conditions are sent to the Exception Queue for Staff investigation and, when required, Manager decisions. Thus, text extraction and OCR provide information for subsequent checks rather than a guarantee that every extracted value is accurate. The OCR component may employ machine-learning-based text-recognition technology to convert visible characters and document text into machine-readable output. However, the proposed system does not use OCR output or machine-learning recognition to determine billing validity, classify payment status, compute charges, or make property-management decisions.

### Rule-Based Billing Processing

Once billing information has been extracted, it must be checked and processed according to defined conditions. Rule-based processing uses explicit conditions and decision rules to classify, validate, and route information. Wu et al. (2022) demonstrate the use of rule-based information extraction to organize domain-specific information, while Jiménez and Corchuelo (2022) discuss business-rule engines for representing and executing operational decisions in a structured manner. Extending this idea to process automation, Bédard et al. (2024) present a rule-based method that analyzes decision logic and classifies business processes according to their suitability for robotic process automation, while Kim et al. (2011) examine proactive exception handling in business processes. These studies support the use of predefined and traceable conditions to determine whether a record proceeds through normal processing or requires additional review. Applying these principles, the proposed system uses defined rules for billing validation, provider and account/reference mapping, duplicate detection, and identifying individual or shared billing arrangements. Records that do not meet the defined conditions are sent to the Exception Queue for Staff investigation and, when required, Manager decisions. These rules, together with the separation between Tenant-reported rent and Manager-confirmed rent status, are system requirements rather than findings established by the cited studies. Digital rent receipts are created only after Manager confirmation. Personal utility tracking remains separate and does not establish provider settlement.

### Shared Utility Billing and Cost Allocation

Shared utility billing presents a related issue because a single charge may need to be allocated among several occupants. Levinson and Niemann (2004) examine apartment energy use when landlords pay for utilities, while Mayr and Souza (2025) discuss coordination and incentive issues associated with shared heating costs. These studies provide context for understanding cost responsibility and consumption incentives in shared-utility arrangements. Nevertheless, they do not establish one universal allocation formula for rental properties or determine how the proposed system should calculate individual tenant charges. For this reason, the proposed system treats the allocation method as a defined system requirement rather than as a formula established by the reviewed literature. For shared occupancy, charges are split equally among eligible tenants in a Manager-configured group. The billing requirements also include reconciliation controls so that allocated charges equal the source billing amount. The allocation formula, rounding procedure, and reconciliation method are described in the succeeding chapters and will be tested during system evaluation.

### Web Push, Rent Email Notifications, and Secure Notification Delivery

After a billing record is finalized, tenants need to be informed that their billing information is available. The World Wide Web Consortium (2024) describes the Push API, including push services and user subscriptions. The World Wide Web Consortium (2022) also explains the role of service workers in handling web events in the background when they are included in the system implementation. Because Web Push involves browser subscriptions and authorization, security must also be considered. Carboneri et al. (2023) examine Web Push implementations in the wild and identify security concerns involving push subscriptions and authorization. In the proposed system, Web Push Notifications serve as billing alerts that direct tenants to the authenticated web portal, where complete billing information is available. However, Web Push does not confirm payment, prove that payment occurred, or show whether the tenant read the notification. Tenant payment reporting therefore remains separate from Manager confirmation.

Email provides an additional channel for rent-related alerts. Klensin (2008) describes Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP) as a protocol for transporting electronic mail. Moore and Newman (2018) explain the use of Transport Layer Security (TLS) to protect email submission and access connections. These standards provide a technical basis for supplementary rent email notifications in the proposed system. Emails will inform tenants of rent billing updates, eligible reminders, and Manager-confirmed rent payments, and will direct them to the authenticated portal. Sending attempts and available acceptance or failure results will be recorded. Email-server acceptance will not prove inbox delivery or readership, and sending an alert will not change rent payment status.

### Iterative Software Development

Because the proposed system contains several connected functions, its development requires an organized and adaptable process. Iterative software development divides development into repeated cycles of building, reviewing, receiving feedback, and improving. Larman and Basili (2003) explain the development of iterative and incremental approaches, in which software is developed progressively through repeated cycles. Inayat et al. (2015) emphasize stakeholder involvement, communication, and requirements refinement in iterative requirements engineering, while Rasheed et al. (2021) identify challenges involving changing requirements, stakeholder participation, documentation, and communication in Agile software development. These studies support the use of Iterative Software Development Methodology in the present study. Although the literature supports this approach, the actual quality and performance of the proposed system will be determined through implementation, testing, user feedback, and evaluation.

### Software Quality Evaluation and Security

To evaluate the proposed system systematically, the study requires defined software-quality criteria. ISO/IEC 25010:2023 provides a product-quality model for systems and software quality requirements and evaluation (International Organization for Standardization, 2023). Guided by this model, the study will evaluate five quality characteristics: Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Interaction Capability, Reliability, and Security. These characteristics provide a basis for examining whether the system performs its intended functions, performs efficiently, supports user interaction, operates consistently, and protects information. Security is particularly important because the system handles tenant and billing information. Republic Act No. 10173, or the Data Privacy Act of 2012, requires organizations that process personal information to use reasonable and appropriate measures to protect it (Republic of the Philippines, 2012). The National Privacy Commission (2016) further discusses safeguards for the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of personal data, while Mohamed et al. (2022) review authorization and access-control strategies that restrict system functions and information according to user permissions. In the proposed system, role-based access separates Admin maintenance and read-only oversight, Staff operations, Manager authorization, and Tenant-own functions. Rent email notifications will use authorized recipient records, protected sending credentials, and limited message content that directs tenants to the authenticated portal. These sources guide the study’s quality and security evaluation, but they do not prove that the system already meets the selected quality characteristics or privacy requirements. The Security evaluation is limited to the defined system functions, access controls, and security-related criteria included in the study’s evaluation procedures. It does not constitute a legal audit, certification, or claim of complete compliance with the Philippine Data Privacy Act.

## 1.3 Significance of the Study

The study develops and evaluates a web-based property-management workflow that uses OCR-based information extraction, rule-based billing classification, Web Push Notifications, and supplementary rent email notifications. It primarily benefits property managers, operational Staff, and tenants and may serve as a reference for future researchers and system developers.

In the global context, the study contributes an applied example of PropTech and digital property management by showing how existing technologies can be combined into one workflow for residential property operations. It also provides a local example of how digital property-management concepts can be applied in a smaller property-management setting. The study does not claim that the proposed workflow represents all property-management environments; rather, its significance lies in demonstrating how selected technologies may be integrated within the defined scope of the study.

In the economic context, the system is designed to reduce repetitive manual activities involved in receiving utility bills, recording billing information, calculating tenant charges, and communicating billing updates. Centralized records and predefined billing rules can also help property managers follow a more consistent billing process. Processing time will be assessed during system evaluation. The study does not establish reductions in operating costs or late payments.

In the environmental context, the system uses electronic utility bills, digital billing records, and electronic notifications. This can reduce the need for paper-based records during the billing process. The study does not claim a specific reduction in paper use because this is not directly measured.

In the societal context, the system provides property managers and tenants with organized billing information. Itemized billing records, defined billing rules, and automated notifications can help make tenant dues easier to understand and communicate. This can support clearer billing transactions between property managers and tenants. However, the study does not claim that the system will eliminate billing misunderstandings or disputes because those outcomes are not directly measured.

## 1.4 Statement of the Problem

This study develops and evaluates an Intelligent Property Management Workflow with OCR-Enabled Automated Billing Classification and Tenant Notification for selected boarding houses and residential apartments in Pasig City.

Specifically, the study seeks to answer the following questions:

1. What development stages are undertaken in developing the proposed system using the Iterative Software Development Methodology?

2. How does the proposed system integrate OCR, rule-based billing processing, exception handling, payment management, digital receipts, Web Push Notifications, and supplementary rent email notifications into one property-management workflow?

3. How does the developed system perform when evaluated using the selected ISO/IEC 25010:2023 quality characteristics of Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Interaction Capability, Reliability, and Security?

4. What implementation guide and user manual can be prepared for the developed system?

## 1.5 Scope and Limitations of the Study

Our study will develop and evaluate a web-based property-management workflow for selected boarding houses and residential apartments in Pasig City during Academic Year 2026-2027. It will cover rent, electricity and water billing, with access assigned to Admin, Manager, Staff and Tenant accounts. Admin will handle account and system maintenance and permitted read-only inspection. Staff will perform authorized daily tasks and prepare corrections, while the Manager will control financial settings, correction decisions and rent confirmation. Tenants will access their own records and permitted functions.

Our system will receive supported utility-email summaries and attachments, together with optional uploads of clear printed-bill photographs or scans. It will use direct text extraction or OCR to obtain billing details, validate them, identify the billing arrangement and calculate individual or equal shared charges. Processing will depend on readable supported documents and verified account mappings. Missing, masked, conflicting or otherwise invalid information will require review rather than guessed values or automatic finalization. Unsupported password-protected attachments will require review and an available supported copy. Routine valid bills will proceed automatically.

Rent functions will include fixed, non-prorated monthly anniversary billing, approved one-month advance handling for the final applicable rent cycle, payment reporting, Manager confirmation and confirmed rent receipts. Utility payment marks will remain personal Tenant tracking and will not establish provider settlement or produce utility receipts. Billing history, actual bill dates, labelled rent estimates, portal alerts, rent and utility Web Push, and supplementary rent-only email will support Tenant access. Rent estimates will not create charges or predict utility dates. Workspaces, basic reports, access controls, system-status checks and backups will support the included workflow rather than serve as separate research objectives. Detailed processing and permission rules are presented in Chapter II.

Our evaluation will use controlled conditions and selected properties. In the local setup, users will need a connection to the server's network; remote access will require a separately configured, reachable HTTPS address. The server must remain running for portal access and scheduled processing. Gmail retrieval and external notifications will require internet connectivity. The study will not establish readiness for large-scale deployment or guarantee continuous public availability, notification delivery or readership.

The study will exclude native mobile applications, smart-meter installation or reading, internet-service billing, payment gateways, automatic bank or provider-payment verification, provider-app access, SMS, predictive billing, model training, autonomous AI decisions, blockchain, maintenance tickets, automatic unit transfers or renewals, legal-contract generation and automated eviction. OCR will not cover handwritten, heavily damaged, severely distorted or low-resolution documents. Evaluation will address the five selected ISO/IEC 25010:2023 characteristics under the stated test conditions; it will not establish universal extraction accuracy, long-term business improvements or complete legal certification.
